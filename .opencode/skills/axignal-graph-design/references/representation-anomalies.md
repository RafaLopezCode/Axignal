# Representation Anomalies

An unexpected map result is not automatically an AXIGNAL error. For example, a
semiconductor organization may appear connected to a hair-salon economic
cluster because AXIGNAL reasoning is wrong—or because real public signals
reconstruct its observable context unexpectedly. Do not silently hide a result
solely because it looks odd.

Make anomalous representation inspectable: identify which graph elements or
relationships are surprising, expose epistemic and temporal qualifiers, and
offer the “Why am I seeing this?” provenance path. Keep anomaly status separate
from canonical error, customer disagreement, and falsehood. A challenge is a
request to investigate, not an edit to the graph. Anomaly detection, authority,
and resolution policy remain governed elsewhere; the renderer must not assign
canonical anomaly truth.

Digital representation adds distinct surface observations (search, generative,
social/public conversation and public reputation/experience). A review
observation records that a source displayed a reviewer claim; it does not make
the underlying experience FAXT. A platform rating or verification marker keeps
its source-native meaning. Experience themes, reputation states, temporal
changes and RepresentationGaps are derived projections, not canonical quality
verdicts. “Why am I seeing this?” must preserve the permitted source lineage,
instrument/version, conditions, time window, eligible sample, coverage and
uncertainty. A volume or duplicate anomaly is not proof of fraud or review
bombing. Respect subject scope (organization vs product vs location), removal
and currentness, privacy, retention and source rights; never infer authority
from public visibility alone.
