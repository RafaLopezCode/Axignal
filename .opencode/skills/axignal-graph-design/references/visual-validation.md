# Visual Validation

Evaluate graph designs against semantic tasks, not node volume or screenshot
appeal alone. Include broad-to-specific economic navigation; direct versus
PATHX interpretation; observed/potential/historical and unknown distinctions;
corporate hierarchy alongside ordinary ties; stale and time-bounded evidence;
focus/recenter continuity; progressive materialization; anomalies and their
provenance; and label-density limits.

Check that users can tell what the map says, what is uncertain, why an element
appears, and how to return to context. Include keyboard-only and screen-reader
flows, non-color differentiation, reduced motion, textual neighbourhood/path
equivalents, and assistive-technology validation. An untested design is not
accessible by assertion.

For performance, record reproducible conditions and distinguish bakeoff
measurements from proposed product targets. Test meaningful tasks with realistic
data distributions; do not infer semantic legibility from renderer throughput.
Visual mappings, label policy, accessibility, WebGPU alternatives, and product
budgets remain open evidence gaps until validated.
