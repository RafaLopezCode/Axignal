# 0. Executive decision

V0.3 makes one structural correction to the previous communication
strategy:

> **COMMUNICATION ≠ PRICING.**

AXIGNAL's main communication system must make a qualified visitor
understand the product, recognize its relevance, desire the outside
perspective, trust the evidence architecture, understand persistence,
resolve the principal objections and want to begin observation.

Pricing is a separate decision surface.

The homepage must not use price to make AXIGNAL desirable. It must make
AXIGNAL desirable before the visitor evaluates price.

Therefore:

``` text
COMMUNICATION
creates:
UNDERSTANDING
→ RELEVANCE
→ CURIOSITY
→ DESIRE
→ TRUST
→ DIFFERENTIATION
→ DECISION CONFIDENCE
→ INTENT

PRICING
then resolves:
COST
→ PACKAGING
→ BILLING
→ LIMITS
→ COMMERCIAL FIT
→ PURCHASE
```

The strategic principle is:

> **Communication creates the need to observe. Pricing determines the
> cost of observing. Never make price carry the product narrative.**

This separation has an important architectural consequence: AXIGNAL's
brand and value proposition remain stable even if price, packaging,
billing cadence or portfolio plans change.

It is:

> **"What must a qualified customer understand, believe, verify and

> subscribing?"**

This is the difference between copywriting and conversion architecture.

# 2. Research basis for V0.3

The external research used for this revision points in a consistent
direction.

## 2.1 Message comprehension is sequential

Wynter's current B2B Message Layers framework treats messaging as
ordered gates:

``` text
CLARITY
→ RELEVANCE
→ VALUE
→ DIFFERENTIATION
```

with proof and friction affecting all layers.

If a visitor does not understand what AXIGNAL is, sophisticated
differentiation is wasted. If they understand it but do not see why it
matters to them, proof will not rescue the page. If they see relevance
but not value, they will not progress. Differentiation matters only
after the preceding gates are sufficiently clear.

V0.3 therefore treats communication as **ordered buyer questions**, not
as a list of product features.

## 2.2 B2B websites must enable buying, not merely describe products

Recent Gartner research emphasizes that B2B buyers increasingly prefer
digital self-service, while digital purchases can also produce more
regret when buyers lack sufficient information and decision support.

The implication for AXIGNAL is critical:

> **The website must not optimize only for CTA clicks. It must optimize
> for informed subscription confidence.**

A click produced by mystery, hype or insufficient qualification is not a
high-quality conversion.

## 2.3 B2B buying is nonlinear

Buyers repeatedly revisit problem identification, solution exploration,
requirements, supplier selection, validation and consensus.

Therefore the AXIGNAL website cannot assume one linear scroll is the
entire buying journey.

The homepage is the **narrative spine**.

Supporting pages are **decision-resolution modules** that buyers can
enter directly, revisit and share.

## 2.4 Differentiation requires proof

Recent Wynter research found substantial perceived sameness among B2B
SaaS vendors. The implication is that differentiation cannot live only
in adjectives.

AXIGNAL should not say:

> "Powerful AI-driven business intelligence."

It should demonstrate a structurally different product:

``` text
INDEPENDENT PUBLIC OBSERVATION
+
CANONICAL ECONOMIC GRAPH
+
EVIDENCE LINEAGE
+
EPISTEMIC STATES
+
REINVESTIGATION
+
TEMPORAL MEMORY
```

The product behavior is the differentiation.

## 2.5 Progressive disclosure is essential

Nielsen Norman Group's progressive-disclosure principle is especially
applicable to AXIGNAL because the product is conceptually deep.

The homepage should expose the minimum sufficient conceptual model.

Advanced epistemic, technical and methodological detail must remain
easily discoverable without overwhelming first-time visitors.

This yields a core rule:

> **Simple first understanding. Deep optional verification.**

Not:

> "Simplify AXIGNAL until its differentiation disappears."

And not:

> "Explain the entire ontology above the fold."

## 2.6 CTA commitment must match buyer readiness

CRO research emphasizes that calls to action should match the visitor's
mental stage.

AXIGNAL should therefore distinguish:

-   **exploration CTA** --- for a visitor who wants to understand;
-   **evidence CTA** --- for a visitor who wants to verify;
-   **commercial CTA** --- for a visitor ready to evaluate plans;
-   **activation CTA** --- for a visitor ready to plant a Xeed.

The page should still maintain one visually dominant next action at any
given decision point.

# 3. AXIGNAL's communication objective

The objective is not to "explain all features."

It is to move a qualified visitor from:

``` text
“I already know my company.”
```

to:

``` text
“I know it from the inside.

I do not have an independent, evidence-backed model
of how it exists from the outside.

AXIGNAL can construct that model.

I can inspect why it sees what it sees.

It will not simply agree with me.

It keeps observing after Day One.

That creates a history I do not currently possess.

I understand what it can and cannot know.

I understand what happens when it is wrong.

I understand what subscribing starts.

I want to plant a Xeed.”
```

Only then does pricing need to answer:

``` text
“What does that cost and which plan fits me?”
```

# 5. The communication operating system

Every public AXIGNAL communication asset should be governed by five
message gates.

## Gate 1 --- Clarity

Buyer question:

> **What is this?**

Required answer:

> AXIGNAL is an independent system for persistent, explainable economic
> intelligence about the observable economy.

Failure condition:

The visitor leaves thinking AXIGNAL is merely an AI company report,
graph visualization or chatbot.

## Gate 2 --- Relevance

Buyer question:

> **Why should I care?**

Required answer:

> Because knowing an organization internally is not the same as
> independently observing how it exists in the public economic world.

Failure condition:

The visitor understands AXIGNAL technically but cannot connect it to a
decision or professional job.

## Gate 3 --- Value

Buyer question:

> **What do I gain?**

Required answer:

-   outside economic perspective;
-   connected economic context;
-   relevant demand, projects and materialized economic activity;
-   capability-specific reach and explainable potential opportunities;
-   non-obvious discoveries;
-   evidence-backed inspectability;
-   persistent observation;
-   temporal memory;
-   reusable professional intelligence.

Failure condition:

The visitor understands the mechanism but sees no meaningful advantage.

## Gate 4 --- Differentiation

Buyer question:

> **Why is this not just another AI analysis?**

Required answer:

> Because AXIGNAL does not end at generated text or a graph. It builds
> persistent governed economic memory, reuses what remains current,
> detects relevant activity, reasons across typed dimensions, separates
> observation from inference, exposes provenance, supports challenge and
> reinvestigation, and explains its conclusions through AXIGLAND.

Failure condition:

The visitor mentally substitutes "I can ask ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini."

## Gate 5 --- Decision confidence

Buyer question:

> **Can I trust the process enough to start?**

Required answer:

-   what AXIGNAL observes;
-   what it cannot know;
-   what evidence means;
-   what potential vs observed means;
-   how currentness works;
-   how a claim can be challenged;
-   what happens after subscription;
-   privacy/security boundaries relevant to activation;
-   how to cancel/manage subscription;
-   where pricing and terms live.

Failure condition:

Interest exists, but unresolved risk blocks action.

# 6. The ten buyer questions that must be resolved before subscription

These are the canonical communication jobs.

They are more important than any particular page layout.

## Q1 --- What is AXIGNAL?

The buyer must be able to explain it in one sentence.

Target comprehension:

> **AXIGNAL independently maps and continuously observes how an
> organization exists in the observable economy.**

## Q2 --- Why would I need an outside view if I already know my company?

This is the central tension.

Answer:

> Internal knowledge and external observable representation are
> different information domains.

The company knows:

-   internal strategy;
-   private contracts;
-   actual margins;
-   internal pipeline;
-   intentions;
-   organizational context.

AXIGNAL observes:

-   public economic identity;
-   capabilities;
-   markets;
-   corporate structure;
-   public relationships;
-   ecosystem;
-   observable competitors/substitutes;
-   representation signals;
-   evidence;
-   change over time.

The value exists precisely because AXIGNAL is not another internal
system.

## Q3 --- What will I actually receive?

Avoid abstraction.

The buyer must see concrete output:

-   an economic map;
-   organization identity and economic DNA;
-   capabilities;
-   markets;
-   corporate structure;
-   observed relationships;
-   potential relationships;
-   economic paths;
-   representation anomalies;
-   evidence;
-   currentness;
-   temporal change;
-   inspectable explanations.

The communication should **show**, not merely enumerate.

## Q4 --- Why should I believe what AXIGNAL shows me?

Answer through product mechanics:

``` text
OBSERVATION
→ DERIVATION
→ FAXTs
→ EVIDENCE
→ SOURCE
→ TIME
```

Primary interaction:

> **WHY AM I SEEING THIS?**

Trust must be generated through inspectability, not anthropomorphic
authority.

## Q5 --- What happens if AXIGNAL and I disagree?

This is not a secondary FAQ. It is part of the differentiation.

Answer:

``` text
CHALLENGE
→ REINVESTIGATION
→ NEW / REASSESSED EVIDENCE
→ UPHELD | REVISED | RETIRED | UNRESOLVED
```

Principle:

> **You can challenge any claim. You cannot buy or edit the canonical
> verdict.**

This is where "Evidence, not agreement" becomes operational rather than
promotional.

## Q6 --- Is AXIGNAL claiming to know the truth about my company?

No.

The website must explicitly establish the observability boundary.

AXIGNAL does not claim access to private reality.

It can say:

-   OBSERVED;
-   POTENTIAL;
-   HISTORICAL;
-   INFERRED;
-   CURRENT;
-   STALE;
-   CONTRADICTED;
-   UNKNOWN;
-   UNKNOWN_PRIVATE.

The willingness to say "we do not know" is a trust asset.

## Q7 --- Why is persistent observation better than a one-off analysis?

Answer:

> **The first map is Day One.**

Then:

``` text
DAY 1
map

DAY N
new evidence
+ disappeared evidence
+ relationship changes
+ representation changes
+ corroboration
+ contradictions
+ historical comparison
```

The key concept is **economic memory**.

## Q8 --- Why start now?

Never use artificial scarcity.

Use temporal reality:

> **Today's map becomes tomorrow's history.**

And:

> **You can research a company tomorrow. You cannot start observing its
> yesterday tomorrow.**

This is legitimate urgency because observation has a start date.

## Q9 --- Is AXIGNAL relevant to my role?

The canonical world remains one.

The projection changes.

The visitor should be able to identify their job:

-   CEO/founder → outside economic position;
-   consultant → independent client map;
-   marketing agency → observable representation;
-   SEO/GEO → external economic identity and representation gaps;
-   strategy → ecosystem and adjacency;
-   M&A → observable target environment;
-   procurement → supplier ecosystem/dependencies;
-   business development → economic context;
-   analyst/investor → observable structure and change.

Do not create nine truths.

Create nine entry points into one AXIGLAND.

## Q10 --- What exactly am I subscribing to?

This question bridges communication and pricing.

Communication must explain the object:

> **A subscription starts and maintains observation.**

Pricing explains:

-   monetary cost;
-   included Xeed/Xignal capacity;
-   billing cadence;
-   additional units;
-   plan limits;
-   cancellation;
-   taxes;
-   commercial terms.

The homepage may link to Pricing but should not need to quote price to
make the value proposition work.

# 7. The central psychological mechanism

The preferred Hero:

> # **¿Estás seguro de saber cómo ve el mundo a tu empresa?**

This is strong because it creates an **information gap** without
asserting a negative conclusion.

It does not say:

-   your company is represented badly;
-   you are missing something;
-   your team is wrong;
-   your competitors know more;
-   AXIGNAL has found a problem.

It asks whether the visitor possesses an external model they probably do
not maintain systematically.

The internal response AXIGNAL wants is:

> **"I know the inside. Do I actually know the outside?"**

This is curiosity with strategic relevance.

It creates legitimate FOMO only in the sense that the visitor becomes
aware of an unobserved information domain and, later, of the temporal
cost of not beginning observation.

It must never become fear marketing.

# 11. Pricing separation doctrine

## 11.1 Pricing is not forbidden from the website

Separation does **not** mean hiding price.

It means price does not carry the main narrative.

Navigation can expose **Pricing / Planes** transparently.

Visitors with high purchase intent should be able to reach it
immediately.

## 11.2 Homepage rule

The homepage may link to Pricing.

The homepage should not require price to explain why AXIGNAL matters.

## 11.3 Pricing-page mission

The pricing page answers:

> **"I understand why I want AXIGNAL. What exactly can I buy?"**

It should not have to reteach the entire category from zero.

## 11.4 Pricing-page information architecture

Recommended order:

``` text
1. COMMERCIAL ORIENTATION
   What is being purchased?

2. PLAN / PACKAGE
   What is included?

3. UNIT OF VALUE
   What is a Xeed / Xignal commercially?

4. PRICE
   Monthly / annual / currency / taxes as applicable.

5. LIMITS
   Observation capacity, portfolio limits, usage constraints.

6. EXPANSION
   Additional Xeeds / portfolio economics.

7. BILLING
   Renewal, payment, invoices, taxes.

8. CANCELLATION / DOWNGRADE
   Clear rules.

9. TRUST
   Security / privacy / evidence / no influence over canonical graph.

10. FAQ
    Commercial objections.

11. CTA
    Subscribe / Plant Xeed.
```

## 11.5 Pricing-page principle

> **Pricing closes an already-understood value proposition. It should
> not manufacture one.**

# 57. Economic brain communication doctrine V0.4

AXIGNAL is not communicated as an index, directory or graph viewer. The
cartography is the cognitive substrate; customer value is economic
intelligence that observes, remembers, connects, compares, detects change and
demand, reasons, discovers and explains.

The two inseparable value pillars are:

| Pillar | Buyer value |
| --- | --- |
| Economic Opportunity Intelligence | Discover what activity may deserve attention, what capability could be relevant, where it occurs and why. |
| Compounding Economic Intelligence | Reuse governed knowledge while current, preserve history and reduce repeated research from zero. |

Communication by professional role:

- **Marketing:** relevant market/project activity, demand and capability-fit
  hypotheses with evidence and explicit limits; no promise of customers or
  guaranteed pipeline.
- **Sales / business development:** economic activity, actors, timing,
  capability-specific reach and potential paths; signals are not leads,
  customer relationships or commercial-fit guarantees.
- **Strategy:** compare markets, projects, capabilities, reach and temporal
  change over governed economic memory; preserve unknowns and alternative
  interpretations.

Every opportunity claim must communicate that it is derived and `POTENTIAL`
by default. A procurement event does not imply a customer, an award does not
imply subcontracting, a capability match does not imply commercial fit, and
headquarters location does not set total market reach. A “Why am I seeing
this?” promise requires an inspectable explanation trace; marketing may not
promise post-hoc generated explanations as evidence.

The compounding narrative is: observe once; preserve valid knowledge with its
provenance, epistemic state and currentness; reuse what remains valid; verify
what may have changed; investigate what is missing; retain history; explain
the result. Compounding error is a risk and must not be hidden behind confident
copy. Low token cost is not the moat. Persistent, governed, reusable, temporal
economic understanding and reasoning infrastructure is the product-moat
hypothesis; no outcome or metric target is claimed here.

Future measurement directions include knowledge/evidence/entity/sensor/
contract reuse, fresh research avoided, marginal research per returning
query, cost and time per useful explainable discovery, provenance coverage,
temporal history depth and stale-knowledge revalidation. These are candidate
measures, not current performance claims or targets.

The subscriber narrative converges with product doctrine: Today shows
relevant activity and change; Explore supports markets, demand/projects,
capability reach and potential opportunity; Evolution shows what changed and
what was reused or revalidated; Evidence explains why a signal appeared and
what is uncertain. AXENT contextualizes and researches when needed but does
not replace evidence or provider-neutral AXIGNAL policy.
